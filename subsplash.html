VCBA — Tech & Budget Evaluation Dashboard

Analyzing administrative labor, platform overlap, and financial stewardship.
BLUF (Bottom Line Up Front)

Subsplash demands high upfront costs ($999 framework fee) but unloads all creation work onto our leadership team as complex "homework." Upgrading Planning Center (PCO) to paid modules resolves system constraints, leverages features our team is already testing, and secures $2,220 in annual savings to fund administrative help instead of platform software.

Subsplash

Verdict: No-Go
$999 upfront tier fee
  • Framework Only: The $999 is strictly a coaching package. They do not build or design our custom app pages.
  • Heavy Homework: Leaves our volunteer teams with a massive manual workload to map, build, and deploy the interface.
  • Premium Premium: Expensive ongoing structural costs without any outsourced build support.

Pushpay

Verdict: Status Quo
$399/month baseline
  • Functional but Isolated: Operates reliably for standard financial management, but leaves user data fragmented.
  • Split Apps: Forces the congregation to jump between separate apps for community workflows vs giving.
  • High Overhead: Locks church resources into a premium price model ($4,788/yr total) for narrow features.

Planning Center (PCO)

Verdict: The Better Deal
+$2,220/yr budget back
  • Unified App Ecosystem: Consolidates everything into Church Center, an application our community already possesses.
  • Low Learning Curve: Our team is already independently testing check-ins and registration layouts; adding paid tiers removes historical friction points.
  • Workflow Protection: Keeps our critical Saturday night audio/visual technical preparation procedures running smoothly in a single software hub.
💡 Strategic Recommendation: People Over Platforms

The friction points experienced using Planning Center are not software limitations, but rather a byproduct of operating on restricted free tiers. Reallocating our budget to unlock paid PCO modules completely integrates our administration, protects baseline setups, and leaves a definitive $2,220 surplus every single year. Rather than spending capital on alternative software, this saved cash flow can fund a part-time administrative assistant or youth contractor to handle routine website edits, data updates, and basic layout tasks—keeping high-level technical leadership free from office desk work.
