Aggregated Community Reviews for Subsplash

The Good (The Congregant View)

Churches uniformly praise the frontend interface. The custom app builder is smooth, and the media storage layout handles sermon audio, video, and streaming wonderfully. For communities prioritizing outward engagement and a premium media footprint, it receives exceptionally high marks.

The Bad (The Administrative View)

Admin staff frequently note that Subsplash is a "walled garden." Its internal church database profiles are shallow compared to Planning Center. If a church uses external systems for complex team scheduling or check-ins, Subsplash does not "play nice," forcing manual data entry or CSV syncs to keep profiles updated.

Technical Assessment & Strategic Recommendation

While Option 2 (Subsplash) offers a beautiful frontend ecosystem, moving our entire infrastructure introduces significant operational complexity. Below is the recommended technical path forward to resolve our current bottlenecks without disrupting our existing ministries.

1. The Core Decision: Retain Option 1

Option 1 remains the most stable anchor for our community. Transitioning to an entirely new system right now will heavily tax our volunteer teams, who are currently focused on mastering our active platform workflows. Furthermore, sticking with our current framework gives us at least a 6-month runway to thoroughly plan any future database adjustments, completely avoiding a messy mid-year financial split for Joel's tax reporting.

2. Resolving the Website Bottleneck (Action Item)

It is entirely true that Option 1 does not inherently fix our down website. However, migrating our entire giving, database, and media ecosystem over to Subsplash just to acquire web hosting creates an unnecessary "can of worms."

📋 Proposed Next Step:

Let's separate the website problem from our church database decision. To unburden pastoral leadership, we should delegate the Bluehost issue to a dedicated technical owner. This person can immediately migrate our web presence to a modern, standalone platform (such as Squarespace or a refreshed standalone host) in a matter of days. This fixes our "front door" instantly, protects Pastor Neil's bandwidth, and allows our core giving and volunteer workflows to remain stable and uninterrupted.
